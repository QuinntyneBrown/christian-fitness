Pattern

Dialogs and overlays

Overlays keep people in context for a short, focused task, then put them back exactly where they were.

WCAG 2.2 AA

Overview

Christian Fitness uses three overlays and nothing else:

  • Dialog: a short task with one outcome (request a booking, edit profile). Bottom sheet on phones, centred on wider screens.
  • Drawer: a long staff edit beside the list it changes (new event, add listing).
  • Menu and tooltip: small, non-modal, anchored to their trigger.

Anything longer, or anything people may want to link to, is a page.

When to use

TaskUse
3 fields or fewer, one outcomeDialog
Long form, staff needs the list in viewDrawer
Content people share or return to (event, profile)Page
Confirm something that can't be undoneDialog with a danger button
Reversible actionNo overlay; act and offer Undo in a toast

Structure

Head (title, optional subtitle with context, close button) → body (alert slot, fields) → foot (Cancel, then the primary action). Below, the dialog's parts are shown in a card; in the product they sit in the fixed .dialog over a .scrim and the page behind is inert.

States

Dialogs and drawers share the form states: default (focus on the first field), invalid (error summary focused), busy (close and Cancel disabled), failed (alert at top, values kept) and success (dialogs only; drawers close and confirm with a toast). Never stack one overlay on another: a confirmation inside a drawer replaces the drawer's footer instead.

Responsive behaviour

  • Under 48rem a dialog is a bottom sheet, full width, up to 92% of the viewport height; its body scrolls, its head and foot stay.
  • From 48rem it centres with a max width of --layout-dialog-max.
  • Drawers fill the screen on phones and slide in from the right on wider screens.

Accessibility

  • role="dialog", aria-modal="true", aria-labelledby the title and aria-describedby the subtitle.
  • Focus moves into the overlay (first field, or the error summary) and is trapped; the page behind is inert.
  • Esc and the close button dismiss when nothing has changed; on close, focus returns to the trigger.
  • Use the native <dialog> or CDK Dialog; never a hand-rolled modal.
  • Scrim clicks close only non-destructive overlays with no unsaved changes.

Content

  • The title is the task as a verb phrase: "Request a booking", "Edit profile", "New event".
  • The subtitle gives context: who or what it's about.
  • The success title is the outcome ("Request sent") and the text says what happens next.
