Pattern

Notifications

Tell people what changed at the level it matters: a toast for a moment, a banner for a condition, inline for a task.

WCAG 2.2 AA

  • Structure

Overview

ElementScopeLifetimeExample
ToastOne action just finishedAuto-dismisses (success, info) or stays (danger, Undo)"You're registered"
BannerA condition affecting the whole appUntil the condition ends or it's dismissed"You're offline."
Inline alertOne form or regionUntil fixed or resubmitted"Your request didn't send"
Inline messageOne field or messageUntil fixed"Not sent. Retry"

When to use

  • Toast confirms something the person just did, or a timely heads-up (rain forecast, event 80% full). Never for errors in a form they're still on.
  • Banner for app-wide state: email not confirmed, offline, delayed messages, scheduled maintenance. One at a time, highest priority wins.
  • Inline for anything tied to a task: the failure appears where the person is looking.
  • Priority: danger > warning > info > success. A danger banner (messages delayed) replaces an info one.

States

Kinds: info, success, warning, danger, plus brand for team announcements (maintenance). Each kind pairs a colour with an icon, so it never depends on colour alone. Toasts may carry one action (Open chat, Undo, Try again). Stacked toasts collapse beyond three; the oldest leaves first.

Responsive behaviour

The toast region spans the width above the tab bar on phones and sits bottom-right (--layout-toast-width) from 48rem; the admin console uses .toast-region--admin. Banners span the full width under the header and wrap their text; the action drops below on narrow screens.

Accessibility

  • The toast region is a labelled section ("Notifications") holding a role="status" polite live region; danger toasts are role="alert".
  • Toasts with an action stay until dismissed and pause on hover or focus; success toasts stay at least 5 seconds.
  • Dismiss buttons are named ("Dismiss"); Esc dismisses the focused toast.
  • Toasts never take focus; anything the person must act on goes inline or in a banner.
  • Danger banners use role="alert"; the rest role="status".

Content

  • Title: the outcome in a few words ("You're registered", "Draft saved"). Text: one sentence on what it means or what's next.
  • Banners lead with a bold sentence, then the detail: "You're offline. Showing what was saved at 8:42 AM."
  • Action labels are verbs: "Open chat", "Resend", "Undo", "Try again".
