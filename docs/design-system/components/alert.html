Alert

An alert explains, in place, why a form or page couldn't do what was asked, and what to do next.

WCAG 2.2 AAStatus: stable.form-alertFocus moves to it on submit

Overview

Use an alert at the top of a form, dialog or drawer when a submit fails as a whole ("Your request didn't send", "Sign-in paused for 15 minutes") or when several fields need fixing (the error summary). It stays until the person acts again.

  • Danger (default): the submit failed. Nothing typed is lost; the alert says so.
  • Info: a neutral outcome that needs reading ("Check your inbox").
  • Success: the form worked and the person stays on the page ("Password updated").
  • Error summary: a danger alert listing each invalid field as a link to it.

Use an inline message for one field or one section, a toast for the result of an action outside a form, and a banner for a page-wide condition.

Anatomy

  1. Icon20px tone icon, aria-hidden.
  2. Title.form-alert__title, 16px semibold: what went wrong, in a sentence.
  3. Body or listA sentence of next steps, or .form-alert__list of links to each invalid field.

Sizes

One size: 16px padding, 14px body, 16px title. It fills the width of the form it belongs to.

States

An alert is static once shown. Its states are the tone, whether it holds focus (after a submit) and whether its links are hovered or focused. Focus is forced here with data-state. Examples carry no live role to keep this page quiet; see Accessibility for the real role.

  • On a failed submit the alert is inserted (or updated) and receives focus via tabindex="-1", so keyboard and screen-reader users start at the explanation.
  • The form keeps every value; the submit button returns from busy to enabled.
  • When the person fixes everything and submits again, the alert is removed.

Responsive behaviour

The alert spans the form at every width. On phones the icon stays top-aligned with the title and the text wraps beside it. Inside a bottom-sheet dialog it sits above the fields, never in the sticky footer.

Theming

Danger reads --color-danger-bg and --color-danger-fg; info reads the info pair; success uses the brand's accent pair (--color-accent-subtle and --color-fg-accent), so a successful form feels like Christian Fitness rather than a generic green. In dark each pair switches to its dark value; no rule changes.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

A failed submit inserts the alert with role="alert" and moves focus to it (tabindex="-1"). The error summary is labelled by its title (aria-labelledby) and lists links to field ids, following the GOV.UK error summary pattern. Info and success outcomes use role="status".

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabFrom the focused alert, moves to the first link in the summary, then on to the form.
EnterOn a summary link, moves focus to that field.

Focus

The focused alert shows the shared 2px ring at --focus-ring-offset. Summary links show the ring like any link.

Labelling

The title is the alert's name and says what failed. Each invalid field keeps its own .field__error linked by aria-describedby, so the summary and the field agree word for word.

Contrast

Danger text
Info text
Success text

Motion

No motion. The alert appears in place; focus moving to it is the cue.

Touch

Summary links are full-line text targets with 4px between rows; inline links inside an alert are underlined and semibold.

Content

  • Title says what didn't happen: "Your request didn't send", "We couldn't save your changes".
  • Body reassures, then directs: "Nothing was lost. Check your connection and try again."
  • Summary title counts and frames: "Fix 2 things to create your profile". Each item is the field's own error.
  • Blame the system, never the person: "Too many attempts, so we paused sign-in to keep your account safe."
