Google button

The Google button starts sign-in or sign-up with a Google account. It wears Google's mark and Google's words, not ours.

WCAG 2.2 AAStatus: newNative <button>Member and admin sign-in

Overview

Use the Google button wherever a person can sign in or create a profile with Google: member Sign in and Join, where it sits above the email form with an or divider, and Staff sign in, where it is the only way in (Google Workspace). It is not a button variant: Google's branding rules decide its fill, border, mark and wording, so it has its own block.

  • Mark: Google's full-colour "G" (.google-g), unaltered: four colours, never recoloured, outlined, cropped or redrawn. It is decorative (aria-hidden); the words carry the name.
  • Wording: one of Google's own calls to action. Sign in uses "Continue with Google", Join "Sign up with Google", Staff sign in "Sign in with Google".
  • Surface: the surface fill and a neutral strong border, the pill shape and the 52px height of the large button beside it.

The mark also comes in two other sizes for notes about the account Google returned (see Sign-in notice): .google-g--sm (16px) beside an email address, and .google-g--xl (40px) on the mocked Google account chooser, which is Google's own page and never built.

Anatomy

  1. Google mark.google-g, 20px (--size-icon-lg), Google's four colours, aria-hidden. Replaced by the spinner only while the page is leaving for Google.
  2. LabelGoogle's wording, 16px (--font-size-md) semibold, in --color-fg-default; one line (white-space: nowrap).
  3. ContainerFull width, at least 52px (--control-height-lg), pill (--radius-full), --color-bg-surface fill and a 1px --color-border-strong border; mark and label centred 12px apart.

Sizes

One size. The button is always full width and at least 52px tall (--control-height-lg), so it lines up with the large submit below it (.btn--lg.btn--block). Its padding is --space-3-5 × --space-5, less the 1px border. The mark is 20px in the button; the 16px and 40px marks are for the account notes and the chooser, never inside the button.

States

Hover and focus are forced here with data-state; live pages get them from :hover and :focus-visible. Disabled and busy are the real disabled and aria-busy="true" attributes. The block has no :active style: pressing it looks like hover.

Both themes

or with email

or with email

  • Redirecting (disabled aria-busy="true"): pressed, the page is on its way to Google. The mark gives way to the spinner and the label reads "Opening Google…"; the button dims to --opacity-busy.
  • Disabled (disabled): while the email form below is submitting ("Signing in…", "Creating your profile…"), so two sign-ins cannot race. It dims to --opacity-disabled and keeps the surface fill.
  • Google did not finish: the button returns to default and a form alert above it says "Google sign-in didn't finish" (Sign in, Staff sign in) or "Google sign-up didn't finish" (Join).
  • Wrong account: when Google returns an account that cannot be used, a sign-in notice names it above the button.

Responsive behaviour

  • Full width of its card or form at every width: the sign-in card (.signin__card), the Join form and the staff card are at most --layout-form-max (448px) wide.
  • The label never wraps (white-space: nowrap). The longest wording, "Continue with Google", fits the 360px phone card with room to spare; do not use longer words.
  • In member Sign in and Join the block sits 32px below the lead (.auth__sso), with the or divider 28px under it; on Staff sign in, 32px below the lead (.staff__sso).

Theming

The button reads only semantic tokens: --color-bg-surface (hover --color-bg-subtle), --color-border-strong and --color-fg-default. In light it is the white button with a neutral border that Google's branding asks for; in dark it follows the dark surface with a light label, the analogue of Google's dark button. The mark keeps Google's four colours in both themes: it is the one place a page carries colours that are not tokens, because the mark must be unaltered.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

A native <button type="button"> (APG Button) that starts the redirect; it is not a link because pressing it starts a sign-in, not a visit. The mark is aria-hidden.

Keyboard

KeyAction
Tab / Shift + TabMoves focus to and from the button; it comes before the email field.
Enter, SpaceStarts sign-in with Google.

Focus

The shared ring: 2px --color-focus-ring at 2px offset, following the pill shape, on :focus-visible only. After a failed or refused Google sign-in, focus goes to the alert or notice above the button, not to the button.

Labelling

The visible words are the accessible name ("Continue with Google"); the mark adds nothing to it. While redirecting, the name becomes "Opening Google…" and aria-busy is set. Never an icon-only Google button.

Contrast

Label on surface
Label on hover
Border on surface (non-text)

Motion

The fill changes over --duration-fast (instant under reduced motion). The redirecting spinner stops rotating under reduced motion; "Opening Google…" still says what is happening.

Touch

52px tall and the full width of the form, well over 44 × 44px; it sits 28px or more from the next control.

Content

  • Use Google's own calls to action, word for word: "Continue with Google" (Sign in), "Sign up with Google" (Join), "Sign in with Google" (Staff sign in). Google, with a capital G, always last.
  • Never "Log in with Google", "Use Google", "Google login" or our own phrasing.
  • While redirecting: "Opening Google…", with the ellipsis character.
  • After a refused account, Staff sign in's mock offers "Try another Google account", which is not one of Google's phrases; prefer "Sign in with Google" again and let the notice above say to pick the work account.
